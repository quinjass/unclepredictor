Private leagues

By Uncle Predictor · Published on 30 July 2026 · Updated on 9 August 2026

A points game - no money betting. Uncle Predictor involves no money, takes no deposits and pays out no cash prizes. Nothing on this page is betting advice.

A private league is a closed table inside a competition - just the people you invite. It exists for what this game is really about: arguing about football with your usual crowd and finally having a leaderboard to settle the argument.

How it works

The principle is simple: predict once, count everywhere. There are no separate predictions per league. The prediction you make on a match counts simultaneously towards the competition's overall leaderboard and every private league you've joined.

Each private league has its own table, ranked by the same criteria as the overall one, and any group-dependent mechanics - where the competition has them - run between the members of your league instead of between strangers.

Creating a league

  1. Enter the competition where you want to create the league (Premier League, Champions League, etc.).
  2. In the leagues section, create a new league and give it a name - the group's name, the office's, the family's.
  3. Share the invite with whoever you want in.
  4. You approve whoever asks to join. The league is closed: nobody gets in without approval.

You can belong to several leagues in the same competition. It's common to have one with friends, one with workmates and another with family, all fed by the same predictions.

Which groups it works best for

Group of friends

The original use case. It works best with a long competition - a league keeps the conversation going for nine months and allows comebacks; a month-long tournament is decided quickly.

Workmates

The strong point is the deadline: predictions close 1 hour before each match and the game sends the reminders itself. Nobody has to go around collecting entries or chasing the forgetful.

Family

Since there's no money betting and no cash prizes, it's a game everyone at the table can play. The scoring rewards intuition rather than technical knowledge - hence the tradition, in almost every group, of someone who never watches football leading the leaderboard in November.

Why the private league format works

In an overall leaderboard with hundreds of participants, whoever sits in 74th place loses interest fast. In a league of twelve people who know each other, 8th place still stings - and one good matchday changes everything. The size of the group is what keeps the game alive until the end of the season.

There's also an effect on risk. In a small league, the rarity bonus becomes far more tactical: you know who tends to play it safe, so you can deliberately risk a result the others won't. In small groups, rarity stops being luck and becomes a read on your opponents.

The rarity percentage shown on each card is calculated across all participants in the competition, not just your league. It's the same reference for everyone.

Rules inside the league

  • Scoring rules are the competition's - a private league doesn't change scoring or deadlines.
  • The league's creator manages who gets in.
  • Members see the league table and, just as in the overall game, everyone's predictions after each match's deadline.
  • When the competition ends, the league stays in your history, with the final standings preserved.
