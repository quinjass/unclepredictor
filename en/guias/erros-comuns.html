Six mistakes that cost points

By Uncle Predictor · Published on 30 July 2026 · Updated on 9 August 2026

A points game, not real-money betting. Uncle Predictor involves no money, accepts no deposits and awards no cash prizes. Nothing on this page is betting advice.

At the end of a competition, the difference between fifth place and first is rarely football knowledge. It's almost always a pile-up of small lapses — and every one of them is avoidable without knowing anything more about the game.

1. Putting off the season picks

The season picks are worth a significant slice of the total, are filled in only once and take a quarter of an hour. They close before the first match and never reopen. Anyone joining a competition already under way can't recover them, and anyone who puts them off until the last day ends up filling them in at a rush or forgetting altogether.

It's the best return per minute in the whole game. They should be the first thing you do, not the last.

2. Missing deadlines

Predictions close one hour before kick-off. A match with no prediction is the only mistake with no possible recovery — it doesn't even give you the chance to fail with style.

The routine that solves this: predict the whole matchday as soon as it opens, always at the same point in the week, and adjust later if there's news. An imperfect prediction that's been submitted is always worth more than a perfect prediction that never got made.

Notifications help — there's an alert when matches are closing within the next 48 hours and another four hours before a match you haven't predicted yet. But notifications without a routine aren't enough: whoever relies on the alert alone ends up seeing it while driving or over dinner.

3. Spending the Joker at random

In the data from the 2026 World Cup, the exact score hit rate in Joker matches was 9.6% — the same as the overall average. In other words, on average the Joker isn't being spent on the matches people know best. It's being spent on the most attractive match of the matchday, which tends to be the most unpredictable. See where to spend the Joker.

4. Writing the same scoreline every time

2-1 and 2-0 together accounted for 27% of all predictions submitted, against 16% of the matches. They're the automatic reflex of someone with an opinion about the winner but not about the goals.

The problem isn't writing them down — they're frequent scorelines. The problem is writing them every time, including in matches where your own reading points somewhere else. And there's the hidden cost: if everyone writes the same thing, nobody gains an edge when they get it right.

5. Ignoring draws

23.1% of matches ended in a draw. Draws were predicted on 15.6% of slips. It's the biggest systematic bias we found in the data, and the explanation is psychological: writing down a draw feels like not choosing. If your honest reading of a match is "this is evenly balanced", the draw is the correct translation of that reading — not a surrender. See how to pick an exact score.

6. Risking the wrong way round

The classic end-of-competition mistake: the player in front takes risks out of overconfidence, the player behind plays it safe out of discouragement. It should be exactly the other way round.

SituationWhat to do
Ahead, few matches leftPlay the consensus — freeze the table
Behind, few matches leftTake risks — the consensus keeps the gap
Many matches leftBalanced, wherever you are

Summary

  • Fill in your season picks on day one. It's the best return per minute in the game.
  • Predict the whole matchday in one go, always on the same day of the week.
  • Choose your Joker match with judgement, not by the billing.
  • Don't write 2-1 by default.
  • Write down draws when your reading says draw.
  • Take risks when you're behind, not when you're ahead.

Data: 104 matches and 4,305 predictions from the 2026 World Cup recorded on Uncle Predictor, compiled on 9 August 2026.
