Rules and scoring

By Uncle Predictor · Published on 30 July 2026 · Updated on 9 August 2026

A points game — no money betting. Uncle Predictor involves no money, accepts no deposits and pays out no cash prizes. Nothing on this page is betting advice.

Uncle Predictor is not a "guess the winner" game. The scoring rewards players who commit to concrete scores, who back them against the crowd, and who know how to pick the right match to double down on. This page explains the mechanics.

The exact values depend on the competition. The mechanics described here are the same everywhere, but how many points each one is worth - and which ones apply - is defined competition by competition. The table in force is always behind the Rules button inside each competition, in the game.

Predict a score, not a winner

In every match you predict a concrete score - not "home win", but 2-1. Your points depend on how close you get:

✅
Exact score
You predicted 2-1 and the match ended 2-1. It's worth the most.
☑️
Correct outcome
You called the winner, or the draw, but the score came out different.
🎯
Near miss
You missed the exact score by one goal, on one of the teams.

One rule that trips up a lot of people: exact score and outcome points do not stack. An exact score hit already includes the outcome; you don't get both. The near miss, on the other hand, does stack with the outcome.

How the near miss works

You earn the near miss bonus when you get one team's goal count right and miss the other by exactly one goal. If you predicted 2-1:

Final scoreWhat counts
2-1Exact score
2-0Outcome + near miss - you got the 2 right and missed the other by one goal
3-1Outcome + near miss
3-0Outcome only - you missed both numbers
1-2Nothing - you got the winner wrong

In knockout ties, the score after regular time is what counts. Matches decided in extra time or on penalties are marked with ★ in the knockout bracket.

The Joker - doubling down

×2 Joker active

The Joker doubles all points for that match, including the rarity bonus. You get a limited number per matchday - and, in competitions with a knockout stage, per round.

The Joker doesn't protect mistakes. If you get the match you used it on wrong, you score 0 - doubling zero is still zero. The Joker is a gamble on top of your prediction.

Because it also doubles rarity, the Joker's real value depends heavily on the match you pick: on a common hit it earns little, on a rare hit it earns a lot. There's a dedicated guide to choosing your Joker match.

Rarity bonus - going against the crowd

This is the mechanic that sets Uncle Predictor apart from a run-of-the-mill prediction game. When you hit the exact score, the game checks how many participants made the same prediction as you. The fewer they are, the more extra points you get, across three tiers:

⭐
Rare
A minority predicted the same
🌟
Very rare
Few predicted the same
💎
Ultra rare
Almost nobody predicted the same

Three rules always apply:

  • It only counts on exact score hits. There is no rarity on outcome hits.
  • The Joker doubles the rarity bonus.
  • The percentage is calculated across all participants in the competition - not just your private league.
The practical consequence: predicting 1-0 and 2-1 in every match is safe but rarely wins competitions. If half the league wrote the same as you, being right doesn't move you past anyone. See the rarity guide.

Deadlines

  • Match predictions: close 1 hour before kick-off of each match.
  • Season picks: close before the competition's first match and never reopen.
  • Past the deadline = 0 points, except for the Default Picks described below.
The 1-hour cut-off exists for a reason: starting line-ups typically come out in that window. Closing before that has every participant deciding with the same information.

Season picks

Before a competition starts you fill in a slip covering the whole competition - who wins the title, who makes the podium, who finishes top scorer, and other predictions depending on the format. They're worth a significant share of the total and close right at the start.

It's the part most people leave for later and the one that decides the most competitions: points you can earn in a quarter of an hour, once, and that anyone joining mid-way can never claw back. The concrete questions and what each is worth are in each competition's rules.

Default Picks - when you forget to predict

If a match closes and you haven't predicted, you automatically inherit the prediction of the AI participant - an automated profile that predicts every match. It's a deliberately weak safety net:

  • It counts outcome only. If the AI calls the winner right, you get the outcome points; if it misses, 0.
  • No exact score bonus, no Joker and no rarity.
  • There's a limit per competition. Once it's used up, matches without a prediction are worth 0.

Leaderboard and tiebreakers

The leaderboard adds everything up: match predictions, season picks, Jokers and rarity bonuses. When two participants finish level on points, the order is decided like this:

  1. Higher ratio of points per matches predicted.
  2. Higher total of rarity points.
  3. More outcome hits.
  4. More exact score hits.

The first criterion rewards whoever was efficient, not just whoever predicted more matches; the second, whoever went against the crowd.

Every competition has its own values

The mechanics described on this page apply in every competition. What changes are the values: how much an exact score hit is worth, an outcome, a near miss, or each rarity tier. Competitions with a knockout stage usually score higher in that stage, and the format also dictates the season pick questions.

That's why the golden rule is simple: before you start predicting in a competition, open that competition's Rules button. The exact table in force is there. See the available competitions.

The scoring tables are configurable. If a value is ever adjusted, it always happens before the competition in question starts - never mid-way, except to fix an error. See the terms of use.
